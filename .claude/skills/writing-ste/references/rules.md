# ASD-STE100 writing rules: a working summary

This is a summary in our own words of the nine sections of the ASD-STE100 writing rules (Issue 9, 15 January 2025, 53 rules). The standard and its dictionary are free on request from <https://www.asd-ste100.org>. When this summary and the standard disagree, the standard wins.

Issue 9 renamed "technical names" to **technical nouns**. Technical verbs stay. Older guides use the old name.

## Section 1. Words

- Use only approved words, or technical nouns and technical verbs of your subject field.
- Use an approved word only as its approved part of speech. "Test" is an approved verb and noun. "Display" is not an approved verb here: use "show".
- Use an approved word only with its approved meaning. Many common words have one approved meaning only. Check the dictionary for each word you are not sure of.
- One word, one meaning. One meaning, one word. Pick one technical noun for each thing and keep it.
- Technical nouns for software include names of programs, files, functions, tables, routes, screens, fields and messages. Write them exactly as the code or the screen shows them.
- Technical verbs include computer processes ("compile", "upload", "download", "parse", "click", "log in"). Use a technical verb only as a verb, never as a noun.
- Do not use slang, jargon or a code name as a verb ("we `fetch` it", "it gets 404'd").
- The dictionary uses American English spelling.

## Section 2. Noun clusters

- A noun cluster has three words maximum.
- Break a longer cluster with prepositions: "the slip status poll interval" becomes "the interval of the status poll for the slip".
- Or give the long name once in the Terms table, then use a short technical noun.
- Use "the", "a" or "this/these" before nouns where English permits it.

## Section 3. Verbs

- Use only these forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, past participle as an adjective, simple future ("will").
- Do not use "has/have/had + past participle" or "is/are/was/were + -ing".
- Do not use an -ing form as a verb, a noun or after a preposition ("by uploading" becomes "when you upload"). An -ing word inside a technical noun is permitted.
- Use the active voice in procedures. In descriptive text, use the passive only when you do not know or do not need the agent.
- Use a verb for an action, not a noun with a weak verb: "do a check of" becomes "check", "make a change to" becomes "change".

## Section 4. Sentences

- One topic in each sentence.
- Do not leave out words to make a sentence shorter: keep "the", "a", "that" and the verb.
- Use a vertical list for three or more items, steps or conditions.
- Use connecting words ("then", "but", "because", "if", "when") to join two related sentences.
- Tables are good for parts, states and data.

## Section 5. Procedures

- 20 words maximum in a sentence.
- One instruction in each sentence. Two actions in one sentence only when the reader does them at the same time.
- Use the imperative: "Run the tests." Do not write "You should run the tests" or "The tests are run".
- Put a condition or a reason before the instruction: "If the worker stops, start it again."
- A note gives information. It does not give an instruction.

## Section 6. Descriptive writing

- 25 words maximum in a sentence.
- Give the most important information first, in the first sentence of the paragraph.
- One topic in each paragraph. Six sentences maximum in a paragraph.
- Use key words to connect sentences and paragraphs, so that the reader can follow the logic.

## Section 7. Safety instructions

- Use WARNING for a risk to people. Maester convention, not STE: also use it for a risk to an investor's money, credentials or private data.
- Use CAUTION for a risk of damage to data, a system or equipment.
- Start with a clear, short command. Then give the risk: what can occur if the reader does not obey.
- Keep warnings rare and specific. A warning on everything is a warning on nothing.

## Section 8. Punctuation and word counts

- Do not use semicolons.
- Use a colon before a vertical list.
- Use hyphens to join words that make one unit ("read-only", "drop-down"). A hyphenated word counts as one word.
- A number, a unit, a code name in backticks and an abbreviation each count as one word.
- Use parentheses only for references and for text that the reader sees on a screen.

## Section 9. Writing practices

- Do not use Latin abbreviations (e.g., i.e., etc., via, vs.).
- Write positive statements where possible: "Keep the key private" is better than "Do not fail to keep the key private".
- Do not use phrasal verbs that are not in the dictionary ("set up", "carry out", "find out"). Use "install", "do", "find".
- Make sure that a pronoun ("it", "this", "they") has one clear noun before it. If not, repeat the noun.
